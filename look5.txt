
# 📘 Pool Pricing Documentation

## 1. Overview

Pool pricing defines how investors’ deposits and withdrawals are valued over time. In a T-bill pool, pricing is determined by the **net asset value (NAV)** of all assets held in the pool, divided by the total number of pool shares outstanding.

The design ensures:

* **Fair entry and exit** for investors.
* **Transparent yield accrual** from short-term T-bills.
* **Resilience** against dilution or overpayment.

---

## 2. Pool Composition

The pool typically contains:

* **T-bills** (short-term U.S. Treasury securities, laddered maturities).
* **Cash buffer** (to handle withdrawals between maturities).
* **Accrued interest** from T-bills.

---

## 3. NAV Calculation

At any pricing event (daily, weekly, or on-chain oracle update):

$$
\text{NAV} = \text{Market Value of T-bills} + \text{Cash} + \text{Accrued Interest} - \text{Fees/Expenses}
$$

$$
\text{NAV per Share (Price)} = \frac{\text{NAV}}{\text{Total Shares Outstanding}}
$$

---

## 4. Share Issuance Models

There are three common approaches:

### A. Floating NAV (Recommended)

* Deposits buy shares at the **current NAV price**.
* Redemptions burn shares at the **current NAV price**.
* NAV/share increases steadily as T-bill yield accrues.
* Mirrors how ETFs and money market funds operate.

### B. Fixed \$1 NAV

* Share price fixed at \$1.
* Yield is distributed by **minting new shares** (balances inflate).
* Easier for UX but less standard in capital markets.

### C. Hybrid

* Combination of fixed NAV and periodic re-pricing.
* Used to simplify accounting in some protocols.

---

## 5. Deposits

* Investor submits USDC (or accepted stablecoin).
* Protocol calculates current NAV/share.
* Investor receives shares = (Deposit ÷ NAV/share).

**Example:**

* NAV/share = \$1.01.
* Deposit = \$10,000.
* Shares minted = 9,900.99.

---

## 6. Withdrawals

* Investor requests redemption.
* Shares are burned.
* Cash returned = (Shares × NAV/share).

**Example:**

* NAV/share = \$1.02.
* Shares = 9,900.99.
* Redemption = \$10,098.99.

---

## 7. Early Withdrawal Mechanics (Optional)

If the pool enforces **epochs or minimum lock-ups**, early withdrawals may trigger a penalty.

* Penalty is deducted from redemption amount.
* Redistribution: either burned, sent to protocol treasury, or redistributed to remaining investors (anti-dilution).

This ensures the pool is not forced to liquidate T-bills mid-cycle at a potential loss.

---

## 8. Revolving vs. Non-Revolving Pools

* **Revolving**: Deposits/withdrawals always open. Requires cash buffer + frequent NAV updates.
* **Non-Revolving (Epoch)**: Deposits/withdrawals only allowed at fixed intervals (weekly/monthly). Reduces liquidity risk but less investor flexibility.

---

## 9. Pricing Transparency

* NAV/share published daily (or block-by-block via oracle).
* Investors always know:

  * Current share price.
  * Historical yield accrual.
  * Next eligible redemption/epoch date (if applicable).

---

## 10. Key Considerations

* **Liquidity buffer**: How much cash is kept aside to handle withdrawals?
* **Oracle feeds**: How are T-bill prices/discounts updated on-chain?
* **Expense ratio**: Management/operating costs deducted from NAV.
* **Audit trail**: On-chain logs of deposits, redemptions, NAV updates.

---

✅ **Conclusion:**
The most robust approach for a T-bill pool is a **floating NAV/share model**, with investors entering/exiting at fair market value. This aligns with traditional capital markets, avoids dilution, and keeps the pricing mechanism simple and transparent.
